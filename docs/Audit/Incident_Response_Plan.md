# Valthoris Incident Response Plan

**Status:** ACTIVE

**Version:** 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines the official Incident Response Plan (IRP) for the Valthoris platform.

Its objective is to ensure that any cybersecurity incident is detected, contained, investigated, resolved and documented in a structured and auditable manner.

---

# Incident Response Objectives

- Protect users and customer data.
- Minimise operational impact.
- Restore services safely.
- Preserve evidence.
- Comply with applicable legislation.
- Continuously improve security posture.

---

# Incident Classification

Incidents are classified into four severity levels.

## Critical
Examples:
- Active data breach
- Ransomware
- Infrastructure compromise
- Privilege escalation

Target response:
- Immediate

---

## High
Examples:
- API abuse
- Credential compromise
- Malware infection
- Service disruption

Target response:
- Less than 4 hours

---

## Medium
Examples:
- Failed security controls
- Suspicious activity
- Isolated fraud attempts

Target response:
- Less than 24 hours

---

## Low
Examples:
- Minor configuration issues
- Informational alerts
- False positives

Target response:
- Scheduled review

---

# Incident Lifecycle

1. Preparation
2. Detection
3. Analysis
4. Containment
5. Eradication
6. Recovery
7. Post-Incident Review

---

# Evidence Handling

Evidence must:

- remain unmodified;
- be timestamped;
- be traceable;
- maintain chain of custody.

---

# Communication

Communication shall be:

- accurate;
- timely;
- confidential;
- documented.

Where legally required, authorities and affected parties shall be notified in accordance with GDPR and NIS2 obligations.

---

# Roles and Responsibilities

- Incident Manager
- Security Lead
- Technical Response Team
- AI Monitoring Team
- Legal & Compliance
- Communications

---

# Lessons Learned

Every incident shall conclude with:

- Root Cause Analysis
- Corrective Actions
- Preventive Actions
- Documentation Update

---

# Compliance

Aligned with:

- GDPR
- NIS2
- Cyber Resilience Act
- EU AI Act
- ENISA Incident Handling Guidelines
- ISO/IEC 27035
- ISO/IEC 27001

---

# Future AI Instructions

AI assistants must:

- never hide incidents;
- preserve evidence;
- avoid modifying logs;
- document every action taken;
- support forensic analysis where appropriate.
